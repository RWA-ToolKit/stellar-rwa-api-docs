# Web App Guide

The web app is the front-end for issuers and investors. It signs and submits every
transaction through the user's **Freighter** wallet — it never holds keys. This
guide walks through the main flows.

## Connecting Freighter

1. Install the [Freighter](https://freighter.app) browser extension and create or
   import an account.
2. Switch Freighter to **Testnet** and fund the account via the
   [friendbot](https://friendbot.stellar.org).
3. In the app, click **Connect Wallet** and approve the connection.

The app follows Freighter's selected network while connected, so reads and writes
always target the same network. Your address appears in the header with a menu to
copy it, view it on Stellar Expert, or disconnect.

<CalloutBox variant="note">
While disconnected you can still browse assets read-only and switch networks with
the network selector. Once connected, the app locks to your wallet's network.
</CalloutBox>

## Tokenizing an asset (issuer)

Open **Tokenize an Asset** and complete the multi-step form:

1. **Asset info** — name, type (real estate / invoice / commodity), description,
   and USD valuation.
2. **Token config** — symbol, total supply, and decimals.
3. **Compliance** — the initial set of KYC-approved addresses and their
   jurisdictions. **Your own issuer address must be included**, because the token
   mints the initial supply to you and that mint is compliance-gated.
4. **Review & deploy** — the app deploys the compliance contract and asset-token
   contract, then registers the asset in the registry.

On success you get the deployed contract addresses with Stellar Expert links.

<CalloutBox variant="warning">
Deployment is several transactions — a compliance contract, a token contract, and
a registry registration. Approve each in Freighter as prompted. If you approve
yourself last, the initial mint will fail; approve the issuer address in the
compliance step first.
</CalloutBox>

## Managing the compliance allowlist (issuer)

From the **Issuer → Compliance** page, select one of your assets:

- **Add an address** — enter the account, choose a jurisdiction, and set an
  optional expiry. This calls `add_to_allowlist` and sets the status to `Approved`.
- **Suspend / remove** — temporarily hold or permanently delete an approval.
- **Block a jurisdiction** — freeze every holder in a country at once.

The allowlist table shows each record's status, jurisdiction, and expiry. See the
[Compliance Guide](/docs/compliance-guide) for the model behind these actions.

## Creating dividend distributions (issuer)

From **Issuer → Dividends**, select an asset and open **Create Distribution**:

1. Choose the **payment token** (e.g. USDC on Stellar).
2. Enter the **total amount** to distribute.
3. Submit — the app calls `create_distribution`, which escrows the funds in the
   dividend contract.

Holders then claim their proportional share. Each distribution shows a progress bar
of claimed vs. total.

## Investor: compliance status and holdings

As an investor you can:

- **Check your compliance status** on any asset's detail page and in your
  **Portfolio**. The app reads `is_allowed` and your `KycRecord`, and shows
  `Approved` / `Pending` / `Suspended` / `Rejected` / `Not Registered`.
- **Hold and transfer** an asset — but only if you are approved. The transfer form
  is **disabled** with an explanatory message whenever you are not KYC-approved, the
  asset is paused, or your balance is zero.
- **Claim dividends** — the app shows your claimable amount per distribution and a
  one-click **Claim** button that calls `claim`.

<CalloutBox variant="compliance">
If your transfer is blocked, the app tells you why — pending approval, suspension,
a blocked jurisdiction, an expired approval, or simply not being on the allowlist.
Ask the asset's issuer to approve your address. This is the compliance gate, not a
bug.
</CalloutBox>

## Transaction feedback

Every on-chain action shows live progress — *preparing → awaiting signature →
submitting → confirming* — and, on success, a link to the transaction on Stellar
Expert. Contract errors are translated into plain-language messages.
